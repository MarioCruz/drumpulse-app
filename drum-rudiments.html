Drum rudiments

The 40 drum rudiments: list, sticking, and how to practice them

Drum rudiments are the basic sticking patterns that every groove, fill, and solo is built from. This is the full list of the 40 International Drum Rudiments from the Percussive Arts Society, with stickings, and a simple way to practice them with real timing feedback.

📖 How to read the stickings

R and L are right and left hand strokes. A lowercase l or r is a quiet grace note: one for a flam, two for a drag. A · separates the pattern starting on one hand from the same pattern starting on the other. Roll stickings show the full stroke sequence for the standard open (double-stroke) version. Rudiment names and stickings follow the Percussive Arts Society International Drum Rudiments; for the official notation, visit pas.org/rudiments.

🎯 How to practice rudiments

  • Go slow first. Play each rudiment evenly at a tempo you can keep clean, often 60 to 80 BPM.
  • Open, closed, open. Start slow, build to the fastest tempo you can control, then come back down without losing evenness.
  • Use a metronome. It shows you whether you rush the fast parts or drag the weak hand.
  • Match your hands. Aim for the same height and volume on both, and accent only where the rudiment calls for it.
  • Use a pad. A practice pad shows every flaw, and it's quiet.

Rudiment questions

What are the 40 drum rudiments?
The 40 International Drum Rudiments are the standard list of snare drum rudiments maintained by the Percussive Arts Society (PAS). They are grouped into roll rudiments, diddle rudiments, flam rudiments, and drag rudiments, and include the single stroke roll, double stroke roll, paradiddles, flams, drags, and ratamacues.
What are the 26 standard American drum rudiments?
The 26 Standard American Drum Rudiments are the original list from the National Association of Rudimental Drummers (NARD). The PAS later expanded the list to the 40 International Drum Rudiments, which keep the original core and add more.
Which drum rudiments should a beginner learn first?
Most teachers start with the single stroke roll, the double stroke roll, the single paradiddle, and the flam. Those four cover alternating strokes, doubles, mixed sticking, and grace notes, and they show up constantly in grooves and fills.
How do I practice drum rudiments?
Start slowly with a metronome and play each rudiment evenly. Practice it open to closed to open: begin slow, speed up gradually to your limit, then slow back down. Keep stick heights consistent and relax your grip. Short daily sessions work better than one long one.
Can DrumPulse help me practice rudiments?
Yes, for the hand wearing the watch. Put the watch on one wrist, play a rudiment on a pad to a metronome, and DrumPulse grades the timing of that hand's hits and shows whether you rush or drag, plus how even your touch is. It measures one hand, so it cannot grade a full sticking pattern across both hands.
