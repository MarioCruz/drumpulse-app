🧮 The formula

kcal per minute = MET × 3.5 × weight (kg) ÷ 200

MET stands for metabolic equivalent. One MET is the energy you use sitting still. Exercise scientists call roughly 3 to 6 METs moderate and anything above 6 vigorous. Drumming moves across that whole range, which is why one fixed "calories per hour" number is never right for everyone.

Example

A 170 lb (77.1 kg) drummer at 4 METs: 4 × 3.5 × 77.1 ÷ 200 = 5.4 calories a minute, or about 324 calories for an hour of playing.

Drumming calorie questions

How many calories does drumming burn in an hour?
It depends mostly on your body weight and how hard you play. With the standard MET formula, a 170 lb (77 kg) drummer burns about 243 calories an hour at an easy practice pace (3 METs), about 324 at a moderate pace (4 METs), and about 486 in a hard, nonstop set (6 METs). Heavier players burn more and lighter players burn less.
How is the drumming calorie estimate calculated?
The calculator uses the standard MET formula: calories per minute = MET × 3.5 × body weight in kilograms ÷ 200. MET is a measure of intensity, where 1 MET is sitting at rest. Multiply the per-minute figure by the minutes you actually played.
What intensity (MET) should I pick for drumming?
Exercise scientists call roughly 3 to 6 METs moderate and above 6 vigorous. Easy pad practice with plenty of stops sits near 3, steady playing through songs is around 4, and a loud, fast, nonstop set is 5 to 6 or more. If you wear a heart-rate monitor, let your heart rate guide you: the harder it is working, the higher the value.
Should I count breaks between songs?
Leave out the long ones. Short gaps between songs make little difference, but a set break spent talking or resting burns far less, so counting it at a drumming intensity overstates the total. In DrumPulse, pause the session for a break and that time is left out.
Is this the same number DrumPulse shows on Apple Watch?
Yes, it is the same formula. DrumPulse uses your weight and its Intensity setting, which defaults to 4.0, and saves the result to Apple Health as active energy with the workout. The app adds the things a calculator cannot: it times the session for you, leaves out the time you pause, and records your heart rate and your timing.
