Drum practice

Drum practice routine with a metronome

A metronome does not make you better by being on. It makes you better when you use it on purpose: one pattern, one tempo, a clear way to raise it, and a way to tell whether it worked. This page is a drum practice routine built on that idea, with a 30-minute session, a five-day weekly plan, and a simple rule for the tempo.

🎯 What the metronome is for

Use the click for three jobs and you will get more out of every minute:

  • Checking your clock. Play something you know and see where you land against the click. This is the honest part of practice, and it is why people avoid it.
  • Building a tempo ladder. Run one pattern up in small steps until it stops being clean, then work at the step below it.
  • Locking in feel. Playing the same groove against a steady pulse for ten minutes is how a groove stops being something you think about.

If your main goal is fixing rushing and dragging, start with the drum timing guide. This page is about how to organize a whole session around the click.

🐢 Pick a starting tempo

Most drummers start too fast. Choose a tempo where you can play the pattern cleanly several times in a row, with loose shoulders and no frowning. For most patterns that is 60 to 80 BPM. If it feels a little boring, you picked well.

Two checks tell you the tempo is wrong. If you are tense, or if the pattern gets sloppy on the third pass, it is too fast. If you can play it half-asleep and every hit sits dead on the click, it is too slow, and you can go up.

🪜 How to raise the tempo

One rule covers most of it:

  • Play the pattern cleanly several passes in a row at the current tempo.
  • Raise the metronome by 4 to 5 BPM.
  • If you rush, tense up, or lose the pattern, go back down 5 BPM and stay there for the rest of the block.

Write the final clean tempo in a notebook or a notes app. Next session, start about 5 BPM under it. Over a few weeks the numbers climb, and the climb is the proof that the practice is working.

📅 A five-day weekly plan

DayFocus
MonRudiments with the click on every beat, tempo ladder
TueGroove with the click on 2 and 4 only
WedFills and problem spots, slowed down
ThuPlay along with a song at its real tempo, no click at the start
FriFree play, record it, listen back, pick next week's problem

Rest on the weekend, or play for fun. Five short sessions beat two long ones.

Practice questions

How long should I practice drums with a metronome each day?
Twenty to thirty focused minutes with a metronome is enough for most drummers, and shorter daily sessions beat one long weekly session. If you only have ten minutes, spend all of it on one pattern at one tempo instead of spreading it across several.
What BPM should I start at when practicing with a metronome?
Start at a tempo where you can play the pattern cleanly several times in a row without tension, which is usually well below the speed you want to reach. For most patterns that is 60 to 80 BPM. If a pattern feels easy and a little boring, the tempo is right.
How fast should I raise the metronome tempo?
Raise it by 4 to 5 BPM only after you play the pattern cleanly several passes in a row. If you rush or tense up after a raise, go back down 5 BPM instead of pushing through. Tempo you can not control is not progress.
Can I practice drums with a metronome on an Apple Watch?
