Practice pad workout

Practice pad workout: 20 minutes, no kit needed

You don't need a drum kit to get your heart rate up with sticks. A rubber pad, a metronome and twenty minutes are enough for a real cardio session that also sharpens your hands. This is the routine, with tempos, and how to make it harder as you get fitter.

🥁 Why a pad works for cardio

Drumming is cardio because it is continuous, rhythmic movement of your arms, shoulders and core that keeps going for as long as the music does. A practice pad keeps all of that and removes the two things that stop people drumming at home: the noise and the space.

What a pad takes away is the weight of moving around a kit and the feet. You make up for it with tempo, full strokes and not stopping. The drumming cardio guide has the general picture; this page is the pad-specific routine.

🎒 What you need

  • A practice pad. Any rubber pad on a stand or a table. Put it at about belt height so your shoulders stay relaxed.
  • Sticks. Heavier sticks raise the effort slightly. Use whatever you play with normally to start.
  • A metronome. A phone app is fine. If you wear an Apple Watch, DrumPulse's Metronome mode gives you the click and grades your timing at the same time.
  • A towel. For the pad if you are on a hard floor, and for you.

⏱️ The 20-minute routine

Everything below is played as continuous notes with no gaps between blocks. Rest only in the "easy" intervals. Tempos are starting points; your heart rate has the final say.

MinutesBlockPatternTempo
0–3Warm-upSingle strokes, eighth notes, light80 BPM
3–7Singles intervals1 min sixteenths, 1 min eighths, ×280 BPM
7–11Doubles intervals1 min double strokes as sixteenths, 1 min singles as eighths, ×280 BPM
11–15Paradiddle blockContinuous single paradiddles, sixteenths, accent the first note84 BPM
15–18PushSixteenth singles, full strokes from the shoulder90 BPM
18–20Cool-downEighth notes, soft, breathing slow70 BPM

Sixteenth notes at 80 BPM are 320 hits a minute. The cardio tempo page explains how tempo and subdivision become hits per minute and how to pick a target from your heart rate.

📈 Make it harder or easier

  • Harder: raise each tempo by 4 BPM when a full session feels comfortable. Add stroke height before adding speed. Swap the paradiddle block for double paradiddles or flam taps. Add a sixth block of 2 minutes at the push tempo.
  • Easier: drop the "push" block to eighth notes. Make the intervals 30 seconds on, 90 off. Shorten to 15 minutes and build back up.
  • Keep timing honest: the moment your sixteenths stop being even, the tempo is too high for a workout. Clean at a slower tempo beats sloppy at a faster one. The timing guide has drills if rushing is the problem.

🏠 Pad vs. kit vs. cardio drumming class

A kit gets you more intensity for the same tempo, because you are moving around it and using your feet. A cardio drumming class, where people play sticks on a stability ball, adds squats and arm movement but does little for your hands. A pad sits in the middle: lower intensity than a kit, far more useful for your playing than a class, and the only one of the three you can do at 11pm in an apartment.

If you have a kit, do this routine on the snare and add the hi-hat foot on quarter notes. If you don't, the pad is enough, and your rudiments will thank you.

Practice pad questions

Can you get a workout on a practice pad?
Yes, if you keep going. A pad takes the drum kit's weight and volume out of the picture but keeps the continuous arm, shoulder and core movement. Sustained sixteenth notes at a moderate tempo, with full strokes, will raise most people's heart rate into a moderate zone within a few minutes. The intensity comes from tempo, stroke height and not stopping.
What tempo should I use for a practice pad workout?
Start around 80 BPM for sixteenth notes and 100 to 120 BPM for eighth notes, then adjust by heart rate. If you are below your target zone, raise the tempo or use fuller strokes. If you are above it, back off. Your timing should stay clean the whole way; if it falls apart, the tempo is too high for a workout.
Does a practice pad workout count as exercise in Apple Health?
With DrumPulse, yes. The watch records heart rate while you play and saves the session as a workout with duration, heart rate and active energy, so it counts toward your Move ring like any other workout. It also grades your timing, so the same session doubles as practice.
Is a practice pad quiet enough for an apartment?
Usually. A rubber pad is far quieter than a drum, and a soft rubber or mesh pad on a towel is quieter still. The sticks hitting each other on flams and the pad stand knocking the floor are the loudest parts, so put the pad on a rug or a folded towel and keep the stand off hard floors.
