Drum cardio tempo

What tempo (BPM) for drum cardio?

If you want drumming to count as cardio, how fast should you play? The honest answer is that tempo is only half of it. This page shows how BPM, note subdivision, and your heart rate fit together, with starting ranges you can adjust to your own body.

🧮 Tempo is notes per minute

A metronome number alone doesn't tell you how much you are playing. What matters is how many hits you play each minute:

hits per minute = BPM × notes per beat

Eighth notes are 2 per beat and sixteenth notes are 4, so 100 BPM of sixteenth notes is 400 hits a minute, twice the work of 100 BPM of eighths. These are rough starting points, and your own heart rate has the final say:

BPMSubdivisionHits / minTypical feel
80eighth notes160Easy, warm-up pace
100eighth notes200Light to moderate
120eighth notes240Moderate, steady work
80sixteenth notes320Moderate to hard
100sixteenth notes400Hard; often vigorous
120sixteenth notes480Very hard; near one-hand limit

The feel column is a rough guide, not a measurement. Heavy, full strokes and playing with your feet raise intensity at the same tempo.

❤️ Let your heart rate set the target

Two common rules of thumb: estimated maximum heart rate is 220 − your age, and moderate effort is about 50–70% of that, vigorous about 70–85%.

For a 40-year-old, that is a maximum of about 180 beats per minute, so moderate is roughly 90–126 bpm and vigorous roughly 126–153 bpm. These are estimates, and individuals vary a lot, so use them as a guide and talk to a doctor if you have any heart concerns.

If your heart rate is below your target, raise the tempo, use fuller strokes, or go to a denser subdivision. If it is above, ease back.

⏱️ An interval session by tempo

  • 5 min: warm up at 80 BPM eighth notes.
  • 2 min on, 1 min easier, ×5: alternate sixteenth notes at 80 BPM with eighth notes at 100 BPM, keeping your timing clean.
  • 5 min: steady eighth notes at 110 BPM.
  • 3 min: cool down at 70 BPM, then stretch.

Adjust the tempos until your heart rate lands where you want it. Over the weeks, raise the tempo only when your groove score holds steady.

Tempo questions

What tempo should I drum at for cardio?
There is no single BPM, because intensity depends on your heart rate, not the metronome number alone. As a starting point, many people reach a moderate effort with steady eighth notes at roughly 100 to 120 BPM or sixteenth notes at roughly 70 to 90 BPM, and a harder effort above that. Check your heart rate and adjust.
How do I know if my drum cardio is hard enough?
Look at your heart rate. A common rule of thumb is that moderate effort is about 50 to 70 percent of your maximum heart rate and vigorous is about 70 to 85 percent, with maximum estimated as 220 minus your age. You can also use the talk test: at moderate effort you can talk but not sing.
What is the fastest tempo DrumPulse can track?
DrumPulse sees one hand, and a short refractory window caps continuous same-hand strikes at about 125 BPM for sixteenth notes. Alternating sticking is not affected, because the watch hand plays every other note. If your tempo and subdivision go beyond what one wrist can resolve, the app warns you.
Should I use a metronome for drum cardio?
It helps. A metronome keeps your tempo from drifting as you tire, and DrumPulse's Metronome mode grades each hit so you can see when fatigue makes you rush or drag. Free Play is fine for open playing or gig-style sets.
